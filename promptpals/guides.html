How to write a better AI prompt

Most weak answers come from vague questions. A strong prompt tells the AI who it should act as, what you need, the background it should know, and how you want the answer to look. Compare "help me with my résumé" to "Act as a recruiter for marketing jobs. Review my résumé below and list the 5 changes that would matter most. Answer as a numbered list."

Start with the task in one clear sentence. Add context the AI cannot guess, like your level or your deadline. Then name the format: table, bullets, short paragraphs, or an email draft. Finish by asking the AI to question you if something is unclear. The Prompt Builder tool does all of this for you.

Three prompt formulas that work

Role, task, format. "Act as a personal trainer. Plan a 4-week beginner routine. Give it as a weekly table." Simple and reliable for most everyday requests.

Context, goal, constraints. "I run a small bakery. I want more weekday customers. I have no budget for ads. Suggest 10 ideas." The constraints stop the AI from suggesting things you cannot do.

Draft, then improve. Paste your own draft and ask for specific changes, such as "make this shorter and friendlier, keep my main points." The Text Rewriter and Prompt Improver tools follow this approach.

Using AI to study without skipping the learning

AI is most useful when it makes you think. Ask it to quiz you instead of handing you answers, to explain a topic with an analogy and then test you on it, or to turn your own notes into flashcards. Always check facts against your textbook or teacher, because AI can sound confident and still be wrong.

A simple routine: read the lesson, write your own summary, then ask the AI to point out what you missed. The Study Helper tool can make quizzes, summaries and study plans from a topic or your notes.
